        05 · Recommendation

        Three moves, in sequence

        Hypotheses, not conclusions. The next section lists what would confirm them.

        Move 01 · now

        Europe institutional to Gulf

        Why first
        Regulated status at both ends already: SO-FIT in Switzerland, CBUAE and VARA in the UAE. B2B only.
        Licence path
        Swiss and UAE routes in place. EU clients need MiCA authorisation.
        Main risk
        Early demand. Around 4 out of 5 Swiss banks have no regulated crypto offer yet.
        Would change my mind
        Little Gulf linked demand in the Swiss pipeline.
        Move 02 · next

        Gulf to Pakistan, UAE leg first

        Why second
        $17.2B a year from Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Jazari as a MENA partner. Fuze has said it is exploring Pakistan inbound remittances. PVARA rules live since August 2026.
        Licence path
        UAE: foreign stablecoins may only pay for virtual assets, so settle via a dirham token or B2B. Pakistan: PVARA, NOC first. Saudi Arabia: no rulebook yet.
        Main risk
        The UAE leg already costs 2.17%. The case is speed for exchange houses, not price.
        Would change my mind
        PVARA licences slipping well into 2027.
        Move 03 · later

        US to Latin America, Mexico first

        Why third
        Top score on size and demand: $61.8B into Mexico in 2025. Modern Treasury covers the US end.
        Licence path
        US: GENIUS Act, OCC final rule October 2026. Mexico: licensed local payout partners.
        Main risk
        The most crowded stablecoin corridor, far from Fuze's base.
        Would change my mind
        US account users already skewing to Latin America.

        Why not the pure score order? The score shows where the opportunity is, and US to Latin America leads. The sequence shows what Fuze can start with what it already holds. If Saudi Arabia publishes a stablecoin rulebook, the Saudi leg moves up.

        08 · Sources and method

        How this was built

        Method
        • Each option is scored 1 to 5 on six criteria against fixed anchors set before scoring. The weighted sum gives a score out of 5.
        • Costs are World Bank Remittance Prices Worldwide averages for sending $200, Q3 2025. Flows are central bank figures, some as reported by the press. Adoption is the Chainalysis index.
        • Regulatory points were checked against regulator text where the site allowed it, and dated.
        Limits
        • Public data only. I have no view of Fuze's pipeline, take rates or plans.
        • Corridor level flows are not published for every route. Where missing, I say so or mark an estimate.
        • Chainalysis changed its method in 2026. Ranks from 2025 and 2026 are not comparable.
        • Some central bank figures are taken from press reports of the official release. They are marked in the source list.
        • Fuze's own public pages quote different volume figures, so this page shows none.
