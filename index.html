    02 · What customers pay today

    The cost to send $200

    What we are looking atWhat it costs a customer to send $200 on each route: fee plus exchange margin. World Bank, Q3 2025.

    What I take from it

    From the UAE, sending money is already cheap, so a new way to move it would win on speed, not price. Where prices are high, such as from Saudi Arabia, there is more room.

    Next question: price shows demand. The next question is where Fuze is actually allowed to act.

    03 · Can it be licensed today?

    A route for stablecoin payments, market by market

    What we are looking atWhether each market has a clear legal path for stablecoin payments, from the regulator's own text, checked 17 and 18 November 2026. Updated 18 Nov 2026.

      What I take from it

      The path is clear in the EU and Switzerland and the US, and clear with one limit in the UAE. It is forming in Pakistan and unclear in Saudi Arabia and Mexico.

      05 · Attractiveness vs readiness

      Putting price and rules together

      What we are looking atEach option placed by how big the opportunity is and how ready Fuze is to act. Numbers show the order I would test.

      The three I would test, in order

        Bands read opportunity / readiness. Tap any shape or name for the detail.

        How the two axes are built
        • How big is the opportunity adds three scores from 1 to 5: how much money moves, how much people already use crypto (Chainalysis index), and room on price (World Bank cost).
        • How ready is Fuze adds three: a clear legal path to permission, partners and presence, and ease of delivery.
        • Bands: Low (6 or less), Medium (7 to 10), High (11 or more). "My estimate" marks a band where at least one score rests on my judgement.
        • The lenses only change what is highlighted and the order of the list.
        What I take from it

        The biggest opportunities are not the most ready. I would start where Fuze can act now and move toward the larger prizes.

        06 · The order I would test first

        Three moves, as hypotheses

        What we are looking atThe order I would test first, with what has to be true and what would change my mind.

        Test 01

        Europe institutional to Gulf

        Why
        Present at both ends: SO-FIT in Switzerland, the Central Bank and VARA in the UAE. B2B only.
        Has to be true
        Swiss private banks want Gulf linked settlement.
        Who is already there
        Sygnum and AMINA, Swiss banks licensed in Abu Dhabi. ADGM register
        Main risk
        Demand is unproven: around 4 out of 5 Swiss banks have yet to establish a regulated crypto offering (IMF, cited by Fuze).
        Would change my mind
        Little Gulf linked demand in the Swiss pipeline.
        Test 02

        Gulf to Pakistan, UAE leg first

        Why
        $18.6B a year from Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Jazari is a partner, and Fuze has reported interest in Pakistan.
        Has to be true
        A dirham token or B2B route works, and Pakistan's virtual assets regulator (PVARA) opens licences.
        Who is already there
        No clear public stablecoin player found. LuLu Exchange and Bank Alfalah announced a blockchain link in 2021. Press release
        Main risk
        From the UAE it already costs 2.17%, so the case is speed, not price.
        Would change my mind
        PVARA licences slipping well into 2027.
        Test 03

        US to Latin America, Mexico first

        Why
        The biggest flow: $62.5B into Mexico in 2025. Modern Treasury covers the US.
        Has to be true
        A licensed payout partner in Mexico.
        Who is already there
        Félix and Bitso, paying out in pesos via USDC, a dollar stablecoin. Circle case study
        Main risk
        A crowded stablecoin route, far from Fuze's base.
        Would change my mind
        US account users already skewing to Latin America.
        What I take from it

        I would test in this order because each step starts from licences and partners Fuze already holds, and the largest, most crowded prize comes last.

        Next question: these are hypotheses. Two things only Fuze can see would confirm or change them.

        07 · Where I would love your view

        Two questions only you can answer

        What we are looking atThe questions public data cannot answer.

        1. Partner pipelineWhich exchange houses, banks or wealth managers already ask Fuze for stablecoin settlement on these routes?
        2. Licence timelinesHow long do you expect a PVARA licence, or an EU licence under MiCA, to take in practice?
        Three more I would check with internal data
        • Take rates: what Fuze earns per dollar moved today, by product.
        • Customer concentration: would a new route spread volume or deepen reliance on the largest clients?
        • Compliance cost per route: what one new route costs to run, and at what volume it pays.
        What I take from it

        Public data tells me where to look, not what Fuze should do. That part needs what only you can see.

        09 · Go deeper

        Method, limits and sources

        Method
        • Nine options (routes money takes between countries, or markets), each scored 1 to 5 on six criteria against fixed definitions set before scoring. Three make up the opportunity, three make up readiness.
        • Costs: World Bank Remittance Prices Worldwide, average for $200, Q3 2025. The bank vs transfer company split is my simple average of the World Bank records by firm type.
        • Flows: central bank data (SBP, RBI, BSP, Bangladesh Bank, Banxico). Crypto use: Chainalysis indices. Rules: regulator text, dated.
        • "Who is already there" names a player only where a public register, company page or press release supports it. No market shares, no judgement on any company.
        • Every figure was checked again against its primary source on 17 and 18 November 2026. Updated 18 Nov 2026.
        Limits
        • Public data only. I have no view of Fuze's pipeline, economics or plans.
        • Europe institutional to Gulf is not a remittance route, so it has no World Bank price.
        • Flows by sending country are not published everywhere. Egypt and Saudi Arabia figures could not be confirmed, so they are not shown.
        • Chainalysis changed its method in 2026, so its 2025 and 2026 ranks are not comparable.
        • This page does not rely on any volume figures.
